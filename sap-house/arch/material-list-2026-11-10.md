# Sap arch rebuild — material list and fab notes for Dean

2026-11-10. Follows the final plan in `2026-11-03-field-notes.md` ("Final plan: zero splits, sloped
frame", 2026-11-05) plus the 2026-11-10 front decisions logged there.

**Conventions:** stations are inches back from the inside of the firebox front. Heights are "below
rim" (down from the top of the pan rail) unless they say "off the floor". "Field-fit" means take the
final size on site.

**Key dimensions:**
- Arch front: 40" W at top, 34" W at bottom, 31¾" H. Rim 32½" off the floor.
- Fire door opening: 27½" W × 20½" H. Its bottom is at 8½" off the floor, the top of the existing
  cross brace, which stays.
- Firebox and ash pit: 29" wide inside, 36" deep. Grates 7" off the floor.
- Pans: finish 1–37, flue 37–97 (7" fins), filter 97–113 (4" fins). Pan 36" wide; fin span 30¾".
- Existing angles: station 59 (top 15" below rim), station 87.75 (top 11¼" below rim). Shell inside is
  about 38½" wide at fin height (chimney end), slightly less just past the firebox.

## 1. Back-bay floor frame (stations ≈48½ → 113)

**Order:** 2" × ⅜" flat bar, 2 × 20'. That covers the 5 bars (≈322") and two ≈38" strips (59 brace and
new brace). New pieces: one 2 × 2 cross brace at ≈48½ (from item 2) and a 1 × 1 × ¼ rear angle
(item 2b). Otherwise the bars sit on the two existing braces.

**The profile:**
- The back of the firebox is vertical up to grate height (7"). The existing steep ramp then rises
  about 6¾" over 9" (about 37°) to station 45½. A short new plate carries it on **at the same angle**
  to a new cross brace at ≈48½ (item 3a).
- From there, **one straight slope runs all the way to the back.** One change of angle, no bridge
  wall, no pockets for fly ash.
- The gap under the fins closes from front to back, then opens again under the shorter filter fins.

**Layout:**
- **5 lengthwise bars at 9 1/16" centers**, with the outer bars about 1" off each side wall.
- **Each bar is one straight piece, about 64½" from ≈48½ to 113.** A 20' stick gives 3.
- **Bricks lay crosswise:** each 9" brick spans bar to bar with about 1" of bearing on each end. Four
  bricks per row.
- **⅜" is decided, not ¼".** The longest span is 28¾" (59 to 87.75). ¼" would sag about 7/32" under
  brick; ⅜" sags about 1/16". The perlite fill under the bars is insulation only and carries nothing.

**Heights and supports:**

| Station | Top of bar, below rim | Brick top to fin bottom | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≈48½ (top of ramp) | ≈14⅝" | ≈5" (7" flue fins) | **New cross brace:** 2 × 2 × ¼ angle standing on the old floor (flat leg welded down), upright leg extended ≈1⅜" with a strip of the 2 × ⅜ flat. Full width. |
| 59 | ≈13⅝" | ≈4" | **Existing brace (top at 15"), extended up ≈1"** with a strip of the 2 × ⅜ flat welded to its upright leg |
| 87.75 | ≈10⅞" | ≈1⅜" | **Existing angle as is.** Bars sit directly on its top (11¼"). No cutting. |
| 97 | ≈10" | **≈½"** at the end of the flue fins, ≈3½" under the 4" filter fins | None (25¼" span from 87.75 to the rear) |
| 113 (rear) | ≈8½" | ≈2" (filter fins) | **New rear support:** 1" × 1" × ¼" angle in the **back bottom corner**: flat leg on the floor pointing forward, upright leg against the back wall. Shim ≈⅛" under the bars as needed. Full width. |

- **The existing floor frame is the long 2×2 side angles, one straight piece each from the top of
  the old steep ramp (45½) to the back,** at about 7½°. The floor sits on them, and the two 2×2 cross
  braces stand on the floor, so the floor is 2" under each brace top (≈17" below the rim at 59,
  ≈10" at the back).
- **The 87.75 angle sets the line.** Bars sit on its top, and the slope runs straight through the
  ½" minimum at 97. At the top of the ramp the gas still has about 2½ times the stack area.
- **These heights assume the flue fins hang 7" below the rim and the filter fins 4".** Before
  welding, **measure the actual fin bottoms with the pan seated:**
  - Fins shorter than that: more margin; build as shown.
  - Fins longer: the ½" at 97 is gone. Then lower the bars at 87.75 by hanging them on clips welded to
    the face of the angle instead of sitting on top. That drops the line about ½".
  - **Hold at least ½" between brick and fin everywhere.**
- **Tack the bars** to the new brace, the 59 extension and the 87.75 brace. Don't run full welds, so the
  frame can grow with heat.
- Paint the bars with high-temp paint before setting.

**Sequence:**
1. Patch holes in the old floor tray, and drill a couple of ¼" weep holes at the low end of each bay.
2. Build the ramp (3a), then set and tack the frame.
3. Pour the 1:4 Portland–perlite fill (Jesse's materials) between the old floor and the underside of
   the bars, and strike it off. Its depth depends on where the old floor plates sit; down to the long
   angles: about 3½" deep at the new brace and 1⅛" at the rear, ≈2½ ft³. One bag each of Portland
   and perlite covers it.
4. Let it dry about a week with the top open.
5. Dry-lay the brick. Set clay pats on the brick, seat the pan, then lift it and read the clearances.
   Mortar only after that.

## 2. 2" × 2" × ¼" angle (2 × 20')

| Use | Cut | Total |
|---|---|---|

| Fire door frame | 2 sides × ≈22" + top × ≈31½", mitered, field-fit | ≈6½' |
| New cross brace at ≈48½ | 1 × ≈38", field-fit | ≈3¼' |
| Spare / weld clips | — | ≈7' |

- **Grates:** 10 bars of 27½" angle set **point down, V open up**, so ash fills the V and insulates the bar, on
  3½" centers. The ends rest on the firebox side ledges and the middle on the new firebrick center wall
  (Jesse's brick work). **Brick must not rest on the grate bars.**
- **Fire door frame (only if needed):** check the existing frame with a straightedge first. If it's within
  about ⅛", straighten or shim it and skip this. Otherwise it replaces the warped angle on the sides and top. The existing cross brace is the
  bottom and stays, though it's slightly bent. Bolt the frame to the front, don't fully weld it, so it
  can expand. One leg is the face the door's rope gasket seals against, so it must be **flat and in one
  plane** all the way round, including where it meets the brace. Shim at the brace if needed.

## 3. 3/16" A36 plate, 3' × 4'

**a. Ramp extension, 45½ → ≈48½:** a short plate, about 4" along the slope × the shell width,
field-fit.
- **Continue the existing steep ramp at its own angle (about 37°)** from its top (station 45½, about
  18¾" below the rim) up to the new cross brace.
- **Lined with 1" rigid ceramic fiber board and 2½" brick (3½")**, the ramp's finished face meets the
  top of the floor brick right at the new brace (about 12" below the rim). The existing ramp and the
  vertical back of the firebox get the same lining. Use **rigid board, not blanket**, on the ramp: on a
  37° slope, blanket compresses and lets the brick creep.
- **Brick on the ramp:** mortar it and start it from a toe at the bottom so it can't slide.
- **The sides in this bay** get full brick up to 15" below rim, then brick on edge at fin height with
  about ⅞" blanket behind. Keep the lining off the fins.

**b. Door liner heat plate:** about 28" × 21". Cut it ½" smaller than the inside of the door's
turned-down lip on all sides, field-fit.
- **Floating mount:** the center hole is a snug ⁵⁄₁₆". All other holes are ½" round, or slotted ½"
  long pointing at the center. The plate grows about ⅜" corner to corner when hot.
- 1" rigid ceramic fiber board goes between the door and the plate, pushed over the studs.
- Mild steel now. A 310 stainless plate can replace it later on the same studs.

## 4. Louver stiffener — 1" × 1" × ¼" angle (from the 1 × 1 × ¼ stick)

Weld it along the bottom edge of the louver (item 6) so the 31" span stays flat.

## 5. Quadrant arm — ¼" × 1½" flat bar, 2'

About 10", pinned to the louver near one end. Cut notches for **closed, ¼, ½ and full open**, and
hook them over a pin or bolt on the arch front. Put it on the **left** side, away from the fire door latch (the door is hinged left, latch on the right).

## 6. Ash damper louver (Dean's stock, or cut from item 3)

The 3' × 4' sheet nests the liner (28" × 21"), the ramp strip (38" × 4") and the louver (31" × 6½")
with room to spare, so Dean can take the louver from it if he'd rather not use his own stock.

- **One piece across the whole ash pit front**, about 31" × 6½", field-fit. It closes against a
  new **1 × 1 × ¼ sill angle at floor level** (F9) at the bottom. Overlap the opening about ½" on
  each side, at the brace and at the sill.
- **Ash clean-out over the sill (brick work, not Dean's):** the first course of ash pit floor brick
  behind the sill is cut to a wedge, rising from the floor to the top of the sill over one brick
  width (≈4½", about 12°), so the rake slides up and out. Cut brick, not a mortar fillet. The rest of
  the ash pit floor stays at full depth (7" to the grates).
- **Hinged at the top on the existing cross brace**, just below the fire door, with 2 heavy-duty
  weld-on barrel hinges (4–6", ⅝" pin), each about 4" in from an end.
  - **The barrels must clear the fire door's swing and its gasket face.** Hang the hinges on the
    brace's lower half or underside.
- It swings up to open and closes by gravity, which is the safe way for it to fail. Fully open, it
  clears the whole pit front for raking ash. Open area is about 150–170 sq in against the 108 sq in
  stack, so it will rarely run full open.
- **Seal:** ⅛" × 1" fiberglass tape (F10), glued on the brace, both sides and the sill where the louver lands.
- **Bottom stop:** the new sill angle (F9). The old loose slotted bar on the pavers comes out.
- **Handle:** a spring-wire handle (F8) on the bottom edge, to one side.

## 7. Fire door latch

A cam or wedge latch that **pulls the door tight onto the rope gasket**. A gravity hook won't do
that. Buy one (wood boiler or outdoor furnace style) or fabricate it. Use the second spring-wire
handle on it.

## 8–12. Liner studs and hardware

- **Cut off the old liner pins** on the door, grind them flush and wire-wheel the inside face.
- **Weld 12 304 stainless ¼"-20 × 2½" studs,** using 9 in a 3 × 3 grid at about 8–9" centers and
  keeping the spares. Use **309L filler** (stick 309L-16 3/32" or 309LSi wire). It's the right rod for
  stainless to mild steel.
- **Stack on each stud:** 1" board, then the plate, a 1¼" fender washer, a nut run down until it just
  touches, and a second nut locked against the first. **The plate must not be clamped**, except at
  the center stud.
- **Check the door's hinge pins.** The liner plate and board add about 35 lb.

## 13. Heat shield, firebox draw-off side (Dean's stock if he has 24–26 ga)

- About 36" long (the firebox length) by about 30" tall, field-fit.
- Stand it **1" off the shell** on about 6 short pipe or tube standoffs, **open at the top and
  bottom** so air flows behind it.
- This is the side that melted buckets and burned the stool. Jesse will IR-check the shell under a
  full fire first.

**Before the rail gasket goes on:**
- The **corner miter on the rim is open**, and the inner angle steps up where it meets the top plate.
  **Grind it flush or fill it**, or it will leak there.
- Straightedge the rail, especially near the firebox.

## 18. Ash pit back wall / bypass (Dean's stock)

- Two burned slots in the ash pit back wall open to the room under the shell, and a strip above them
  opens into the gas path. Together they let air bypass the fire.
- **Close them with 3/16" cover plates**, plus any other holes in the shell bottom. This also keeps
  mice out of the perlite fill.
- Jesse's crew then lays a mortared firebrick wall in front, 29" wide × 7" tall, kept clear of the
  rear grate support.

## Safety

- Some panels are galvanized, which gives off zinc fumes when cut or welded. Ventilate and use a
  respirator.
