# Session Summary — Sap house extension design and arch field measurements

Two pieces of sugarhouse work: the design of the east awning and north porch (drawn as an ARES
Commander script), and field measurements for the evaporator arch rebuild planned on 2026-10-30.
Working files are untracked in `updates/` (see the end of this note).

## Arch rebuild: field update

Follows `2026-10-30.md`. Measured on site 2026-11-03.

### Measurements

- **Rim opening:** 34¼" inside edge to inside edge (the 10-30 plan had 34.5). The rim is 3" angle cut
  back to fit the current pan; the outside shell sits about 2½" outboard of the rim edge.
- **Pan:** 36" full width. Probably not the original pan, since the rim was cut down to fit it.
  The pan bears about ⅞" on each side of the rim.
- **Flue pan fin span:** 30¾" outside to outside.
- **Shell width 7" below rim (bottom of fins):** 38½" at the chimney end. Just past the brace under the
  finish pan it is slightly under 38½", because the side walls there are heavier steel.
- **Layout:** the finish pan sits over the firebox and runs 3 ft (stations 1–37). The flue pan starts
  after it, with a cross brace under the finish pan.

### Fin clearance check

The 10-30 rule: width at fin height − 4.5 ≥ fin span + 1. With 30¾" fins that needs at least 36¼" of
shell width 7" below the rim. 38½" passes with 2¼" to spare. Only the zone from about station 37 back
needs the check; the finish pan has no fins.

### Decisions

- **37–59 bay:** full 2½" brick on 1" blanket up to the top of the first angle (15" below rim), as
  planned; **split brick above that** at fin height. Full brick at fin height would need 38¾" and
  doesn't fit.
- **Last two bays (59–87.75, 87.75–113):** split brick on the bottom and both sides.
  - Sides: 1" insulation + 1¼" splits. Because the fin check passes, use **rigid board** behind the
    side splits (the 10-30 option) instead of blanket.
  - Bottom: unchanged — splits on the new 3/16" trapezoid plates over the blanket-filled void, aiming
    for 6.5" under the fins at 59, 2.8" at 87.75, 2.5" at 97 and 4.5" at 113. Dry-fit before mortaring.

### Ash pit

- The steel center support under the grates (36½ × 7 × ⅜) is burned through along its top edge
  mid-length, where it sits under the coal bed with primary air washing past it. The steel ash pit
  floor is badly warped and lifts behind the front sill, leaving an open air gap.
- The arch sits directly on the paver floor (no legs).
- **Decision:** cut out the floor steel and replace both with firebrick.
  - **Floor:** about 26 firebrick laid flat across 29" × 36". **Lift the pavers inside the arch and
    bed the brick at the old floor level**, keeping the 7" grate-to-floor depth (ash storage, air
    under all bars, cooler bars) and a flush threshold at the sill. Mortar tight to the sill to close
    the gap. Don't go shallower than 4½".
  - **Center wall:** one flat course plus one course on edge = 7", narrow 2½" top under the bars,
    about 8 brick. No notches; the side notches still locate the bars.
  - Before cutting: if the floor plate is welded to the side walls, leave a 2–3" perimeter strip or
    add cross ties so the shell stays square.

### Front section rebuild (shop build)

- **Condition:** the fire door's liner is gone (only the stud grid remains), so the plate takes
  direct fire. The frame angle is warped, with a daylight gap and only scraps of rope gasket. The
  ash pit front is open behind a loose sill bar, so there's no primary air control.
- **Ash door with damper:** hinged across the 29" pit front on rope gasket, with **two sliding gates,
  one per ash channel**, about 4" × 12" each (~96 sq in open, close to the 108 sq in stack area).
  This is the main fire control from the 10-30 plan.
- **Fire door:** reline the big door on its stud pattern (1–2" ceramic fiber board or castable,
  stainless studs), with rope gasket in a channel and a cam latch. **Cut an opening in the middle
  and mount a used wood-stove door with ceramic glass** (Robax/Neoceram, not tempered; new glass and
  gasket). Its built-in air control gives metered overfire air and keeps the glass clean, replacing
  the overfire slide and the separate glass frame in the 10-30 plan. Stiffen the cut-out with a
  welded frame, leave a 4–5" border of plate, and check the hinge pins for the added weight. The big
  door still opens for loading.
- **Frame:** rebuild in ¼" angle, bolted rather than fully welded so it can expand, with the lining
  brought flush to the opening so no frame steel sees flame.
- **Measure before drawing it:** door opening W×H; door plate size, thickness and stud spacing; ash
  pit front opening W×H; floor to front grate support; existing frame angle size; hinge side; and the
  chosen stove door's frame size and cut-out.

### Quantity changes

- Side splits add roughly **45–50 splits**. If the 10-30 count of 106 didn't include sides, plan on
  about 150–155.
- Rigid board on the sides adds about 13 sq ft beyond the 3 sheets planned for the rear wall.

### Still open (arch)

- Exact shell width just past the brace (anything ≥ 36¼" passes).
- Filtration pan fin span (4" fins), measured separately from the flue pan.
- Plate-level widths at each angle and the rear lip, and clear lengths between angles (needed to cut
  the plates).
- Firebox side ledge width (needs about 2½"); log length against about 24" at the base.
- Door size for the liner and glass frame.
- Pan seal: with only ⅞" of bearing per side, confirm the rail gasket fits on the cut-back rim and the
  rim is flat and level along its length.
- How the side splits are held: they stand on the floor splits; confirm they don't need clips or pins
  against the rigid board over the 11–15" height.
- Carried from 10-30: stack checks (bands, support, hood height, clearances), the 12UT-36 listing and
  the local official's opinion, hood seam fill test, sight tube, low-sap procedure.

## Sap house extension: east awning and north porch

Design worked out in ARES Commander. The plan is drawn from the magicplan measurements, corrected on
site.

### Existing building (as measured)

- 159½" × 363" outside, 155½" × 359" inside, 2" board-and-batten siding.
- 7½" posts; post bays from the north 108", 137", 84" clear.
- Long beams 7½" × 8", 108" to 116" (bottom to top); cross beams top at 94".
- 4x6 rafters at 24" o.c. (confirmed on site) under 2" battens and cedar shakes, about 8.6:12, 172" to
  the bottom of the rafters at the ridge, 11" overhangs.

### Design

- **East awning (Phase 1, enclosed):** 6' deep, 3:12, from the post at the south edge of the center
  bay north to the north wall line (the SE bank limits it). The access road is on the west.
  - 2x10 rafters at 24" sit on top of the existing long beam and are **sistered to the existing 4x6s**
    with two ¼" structural screws each. They're birdsmouthed at both bearings (engineer).
  - Outside beam: double 2x10 let 2" into the 6x6 posts, 1" proud, on three 87⅝" bays.
  - PT kickboard let in the same way; board and batten between; paver floor.
  - Remove one course of shakes and battens and fit a heavy kick flashing over the pitch break, which
    lands about 3" outside the siding.
  - Headroom 7'-2¼" under the outside beam; eave tails 7'-4¾".
- **North porch (Phase 2):** a lower, separate gable centered on the building, 8' to the front post
  line.
  - 3:12 skirts out 6' past each wall; the east skirt continues the awning roof unbroken. 5:12 center
    gable with the ridge at 146⅞" (bottom of rafter), about 14" under the main rafters.
  - Asphalt shingles on 2x6 tongue-and-groove decking; rafter ties at 10'-6".
  - Four front posts on 20" stone piers with bluestone caps at 30"; 13'-6" center opening.
  - Trim the main north rake from about 46" off center out to each corner.
- **Drainage:** French drain from the SE high point, up the east drip line and along the south end,
  falling to the existing catch basin near the NW corner. A 12" × 43" trench drain runs from the yard
  hydrant (south end of the center bay) to the arch, draining to the south leg.
- **Footings:** 4' deep, 10" sonotubes on spread bases (18" bell or 20×20×8 pad, 24" at the NE corner
  post, which also carries the porch). Engineer to confirm soil bearing.

### Still open (extension)

- Catch basin location (measure from the NW corner).
- Engineer: footing sizes, porch break beams, sister screw spec.
- Trench drain outflow: storm is fine for hydrant water, but not for pan-wash or acid/niter cleaner.
- Building permit (Town of Esopus) for the awning and porch.
- Snow guards on the main roof above the awning: snow sliding off the 8.6:12 shakes lands on a 3:12 roof.
- Access to the enclosed awning: as drawn, its only door is the existing east sliding door from inside
  the sap house.
